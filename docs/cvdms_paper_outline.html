1.1 Novelty positioning relative to Chen & Van Dyck 1997

Chen and Van Dyck[1] originally formulated the slice-transmission-operator (STO) matrix and derived the forward-scattering coefficient (FSC) and backscattering coefficient (BSC) operators that motivate the present work. Their treatment was analytical and did not provide adaptive truncation diagnostics, antialias control compatible with FFT/finite-difference Laplacians, or a flux-conserving Fresnel BSC suitable for routine TEM-scale grids. The novelty of the present manuscript is therefore not the dual-Taylor structure itself, but the conversion of an open-form analytical scheme into a falsifiable computational framework with three measurable physical quantities: (i) the per-pixel divergence ratio that maps to the local scattering mean free path, (ii) a stagnation count that detects regimes in which slice projection itself fails, and (iii) a Fresnel-form BSC operator whose unitarity residual $|T|^2+|R|^2-1$ is directly observable. This re-frames Chen & Van Dyck's open scheme as a diagnostic probe of when projected-slice multislice approximations are controlled.

2. Abstract & Significance

2.1 Abstract (target ≤150 words)

Multiple elastic scattering of high-energy electrons in solids is conventionally simulated by Lie–Trotter splitting of the projected-slice scattering operator, an approximation whose commutator error is not exposed during the simulation itself. Here we show that direct Taylor exponentiation of the projected scattering operator $\hat{K}=\sigma V+\nabla^2/(4\pi K_0)$, evaluated through a coefficient-scaled square-root cascade and tracked pixel-by-pixel, yields three measurable physical quantities — a divergence ratio, a stagnation count, and a Fresnel backscattering residual $|T|^2+|R|^2-1$ — that quantify when slice-based propagation is controlled. We validate the framework against analytic limits, against $\Delta z$-refined Fourier multislice for SrTiO$_3$, Si, and Au at 30–300 keV, and across a CuPy and a C++/CUDA implementation. The diagnostics define a physically interpretable operating envelope for projected-potential multislice simulation and identify regimes in which finer slicing or non-projected physics is required.

2.2 One-sentence significance statement (Comms Phys "Editor's summary")

A pixel-resolved series formulation of the projected-potential scattering operator turns multislice convergence from an implicit numerical assumption into a directly measurable physical quantity, defining when conventional slice-based electron-scattering simulations are physically controlled.

3. Introduction

~700–900 words. Open with the physics problem; show why current splitting-based multislice is uncontrolled in specific regimes; state the three measurable diagnostics introduced here; close with the four contributions.

3.1 The multiple-scattering problem at high energies

  • Fast electrons (30–300 keV) interact with crystals via cross-sections orders of magnitude larger than X-rays or neutrons; the Born approximation fails for all but the thinnest specimens[6].
  • The relativistically corrected Schrödinger-like equation $\nabla^2\Psi+(2\pi\hat{K})^2\Psi=0$ governs the propagation, with $\hat{K}=K_0\sqrt{1+\nabla_\perp^2/(2\pi K_0)^2+\sigma U/(\pi K_0)}$[1].
  • Practical simulation requires a slice decomposition; within each slice the potential is projected onto a transverse plane.
  • The conventional multislice scheme[6,7] approximates the slice exponential as $\exp(i\Delta z\,\hat{A})\exp(i\Delta z\,\hat{B})$ — a Lie–Trotter splitting whose commutator error is $\mathcal{O}(\Delta z^2[\hat{A},\hat{B}])$ and is not directly observable during the run.

3.2 Limits of the splitting approach

  • The neglected commutator can become large for strong potentials or thick slices: for 30 keV electrons ($\lambda\!\approx\!0.0698\,\text{Å}$) the per-slice scattering phase $\sigma V_\text{proj}\Delta z$ can exceed $\pi$ for heavy elements at $\Delta z>0.4$ Å, violating the thin-slice premise.
  • Earlier Taylor-series treatments[1,8] avoided splitting but lacked adaptive truncation; truncation was heuristic.
  • Existing high-accuracy alternatives — full multislice with refined slicing[2,3,4], Bloch-wave methods[6], and PRISM[3] — control accuracy through global parameters rather than per-pixel observables.

3.3 The CVDMS approach

  • The Chen–Van Dyck Multislice (CVDMS) framework expands the projected forward-scattering operator $\exp(i\Delta z\,\hat{K})$ directly via a Taylor series, with $\hat{K}\psi=\sigma V\psi+\nabla_\perp^2\psi/(4\pi K_0)$.
  • Each Taylor power $\hat{K}^n\psi$ is evaluated through an inner square-root binomial cascade with coefficients $c_1=1$, $c_n=(0.5-n+1)\lambda/(\pi n)$, $n>1$, in scaled form $\text{cur}_n=c_n\,\hat{K}(\text{cur}_{n-1})$ so that coefficient damping propagates into all higher orders.
  • Three measurable physical quantities emerge:
    • The divergence ratio $r=\sum|w|/\sum|\psi_{\text{exit}}|$ measures the local strength of the scattering operator's nonlinearity within one slice.
    • The K-series stagnation count identifies pixels for which the inner cascade neither converges nor decays — the local slice is too thick relative to the scattering mean free path.
    • The Fresnel BSC residual $|T|^2+|R|^2-1$ is the directly observable unitarity error of the inter-slice interface treatment.
Central thesis: The convergence diagnostics of the CVDMS operator expansion are not merely numerical stopping criteria; they are local probes of the competition between potential scattering and transverse diffraction, and therefore identify the physical operating envelope of projected-potential multislice simulations.

3.4 Contributions

  1. Physical: Three measurable quantities ($r$, stagnation count, $|T|^2+|R|^2-1$) that quantify when projected-slice multislice is controlled.
  2. Mathematical: A coefficient-scaled inner cascade that prevents apparent divergence of the Taylor sum in float32 arithmetic for typical TEM parameters, derived from linearity of $\hat{K}$.
  3. Algorithmic: Pixel-wise convergence control combining divergence-ratio truncation, stagnation detection, $\frac{2}{3}$-Nyquist antialias bandlimiting, and a flux-conserving Fresnel BSC consistent with continuity of $\psi$ and $\partial_z\psi$.
  4. Computational: A CuPy and a C++/CUDA implementation with fused finite-difference and cuFFT Laplacians and packed convergence readback, validated against abTEM Fourier multislice on three crystal classes.

4. Results

4.1 Forward-scattering operator passes physical self-tests (Fig. 1)

  • Unitarity in vacuum. For $V=0$, both the FD and FFT paths reproduce unitary Fresnel propagation within the precision expected from complex64 arithmetic.
  • Unitarity in matter. SrTiO$_3$ at 30 keV, $\Delta z=0.4$ Å: per-slice flux deviation and the Fresnel BSC residual $|T|^2+|R|^2-1$ are reported as direct unitarity probes.
  • Analytic limits. The vacuum Fresnel propagator (Gaussian wave-packet), the weak-phase limit $\psi\!\approx\!\exp(i\sigma V_\text{proj})$, and the homogeneous-potential plane-wave solution are recovered.

4.2 Convergence is controlled and discretization-independent (Fig. 2)

  • Slice refinement. $\Delta z\in\{0.4,0.2,0.1,0.05\}$ Å with proportional slice counts: NCC between consecutive refinements approaches 1; discretization error scales as $\mathcal{O}(\Delta z^2)$.
  • Threshold refinement. Convergence threshold $\varepsilon\in[10^{-5},10^{-9}]$: Taylor order and inner K-iterations grow logarithmically; observables stabilize.
  • Backend cross-validation. Python fused kernel vs. C++ CUDA: NCC $> 1-10^{-6}$ for identical inputs; FD vs. FFT Laplacian agreement within float32 precision for bandlimited potentials.

4.3 A physical regime map separates convergent, conditional, and divergent slices (Fig. 3)

  • The $(\Delta z, V_\text{max})$ plane partitions into three regions:
    • Convergent: $\sigma V_\text{max}\Delta z\ll 1$; series converges within ~5 outer terms; NCC vs. refined reference $> 0.99$.
    • Conditional: $\sigma V_\text{max}\Delta z\sim 1$; convergence requires >10 terms, stagnation detection becomes critical.
    • Divergent: $\sigma V_\text{max}\Delta z\gg 1$; the K-series fails to converge for any threshold; CVDMS warns the user that the projected-slice approximation is no longer controlled.
  • Boundaries are reported using dimensionless control variables ($\sigma V_\text{max}\Delta z$, $\lambda V_\text{max}$, $\Delta z/\ell_\text{mfp}$), not raw numerical parameters.

4.4 Agreement and disagreement with conventional Fourier multislice (Fig. 4)

  • SrTiO$_3$ [001], Si [110], Au [001] at 30, 100, 300 keV: NCC vs. $\Delta z$-refined abTEM Fourier multislice[2], integrated CBED-disk intensities, high-angle scattering tails.
  • Disagreement regimes are identified and physically interpreted (commutator-sensitive zones, heavy-element tails) rather than averaged into a single error metric.
  • The defensible claim is that CVDMS provides an independently controlled approximation whose convergence diagnostics expose errors hidden in fixed-splitting workflows.

4.5 Bandwidth control by per-iteration antialiasing (Fig. 5)

  • Without antialiasing, the K-series power spectrum saturates the Nyquist boundary by $n\!=\!3$; the resulting aliased low-$k$ noise is what previously masqueraded as "divergence" of the bare Taylor sum.
  • The $\frac{2}{3}$-Nyquist aperture applied after each $\hat{K}$ application contains the bandwidth and stabilizes the K-series; the FD and FFT Laplacian backends share the same aperture for fair comparison.

4.6 Fresnel BSC restores the unitarity that SBA violates (Fig. 6)

  • The single-backscattering approximation (SBA) treats the interface reflection as a real amplitude reduction $T=1-B$, which gives $|T|^2>1$ when $B<0$.
  • The Fresnel form derived from continuity of $\psi$ and $\partial_z\psi$ yields $R=(K_\text{cur}-K_\text{next})/(K_\text{cur}+K_\text{next})$, $T=\sqrt{1-|R|^2}$, with $|T|^2+|R|^2=1$ identically.
  • I/I$_0$ vs. thickness for (no BSC, SBA, Fresnel) on a SrTiO$_3$ / vacuum interface and a SrTiO$_3$/Si bilayer: SBA can amplify by up to ~17%; Fresnel BSC stays within $10^{-7}$ of unity.

5. Discussion

~600–900 words. Interpret the diagnostics in scattering-physics terms, situate the work against splitting-based multislice and Bloch-wave methods, and state the operating envelope.

5.1 Physical interpretation of the divergence ratio

The divergence ratio $r=\sum|w|/\sum|\psi_{\text{exit}}|$ measures whether the next Taylor term remains a controlled correction to the accumulated wave. When $r$ exceeds unity, the local scattering term $\sigma V\psi$ outpaces the diffractive smoothing $\nabla_\perp^2\psi/(4\pi K_0)$, and the inner cascade can no longer be damped by the binomial coefficients $c_n$. This maps directly to the local scattering mean free path $\ell_\text{mfp}=1/(\sigma V_\text{rms})$: the K-series fails precisely when $\Delta z\!\gtrsim\!\ell_\text{mfp}$, i.e. when the projected-slice premise itself breaks.

5.2 Relation to Lie–Trotter splitting and Bloch-wave methods

Conventional Fourier multislice[6,7,2] is a Lie–Trotter splitting that exactly conserves $\Vert\psi\Vert$ and is well-validated in abTEM[2], prismatic[3], and MULTEM[4]. Its commutator error $\mathcal{O}(\Delta z^2[\sigma V,\nabla_\perp^2/(4\pi K_0)])$ is typically managed by reducing $\Delta z$ until the result no longer changes — an indirect convergence test that requires running the same simulation many times. CVDMS is not a "better splitting"; it directly exponentiates $\hat{K}$ and exposes the would-be commutator error as the per-slice divergence ratio. Bloch-wave methods[6] control accuracy through the number of beams retained, but require periodic potentials and become expensive for HRTEM-scale beam counts. CVDMS retains the geometric flexibility of multislice while making the convergence quantity locally observable.

5.3 Implications for quantitative TEM simulation

  • The $(\Delta z, V_\text{max})$ phase diagram provides a decision tree: which specimen / energy / thickness combinations are safe at a given $\Delta z$ before the run completes.
  • For heavy elements at low voltage (e.g., Au at 30 keV), float32 cancellation places a precision floor on any algorithm — this is not a CVDMS limitation but should be reported, with float64 K-series as the recommended workaround.
  • The Fresnel BSC formulation generalizes interface reflection at slice boundaries and could be extended to vacuum/specimen and specimen/substrate interfaces in heterostructure modelling.

5.4 Limitations and outlook

  • Elastic only. Inelastic processes (plasmons, phonons[9]) require separate treatment; CVDMS provides the elastic foundation.
  • Projected potential premise. The diagnostics indicate when projection itself fails; they do not repair it. In divergent regimes, fully three-dimensional methods[10] are required.
  • Cost. Per-slice cost is higher than Lie–Trotter multislice when both run at converged settings; the value of CVDMS is the ability to know the simulation is converged.
  • Future work. Mixed-precision K-series, adaptive $\Delta z$ selection driven by the live divergence ratio, and integration with ptychographic and 4D-STEM workflows[5].

6. Methods

Comms Phys "Methods" appears after Discussion and is exempt from the main-text word count. It must be detailed enough for an independent group to reproduce every quantitative claim.

6.1 Governing equation and operator $\hat{K}$

The relativistically corrected scalar wave equation for high-energy electrons reads[1]

$$ \nabla^2\Psi(\mathbf{r}) + \big[2\pi\hat{K}(\mathbf{r})\big]^2\Psi(\mathbf{r}) = 0, \qquad \hat{K}(\mathbf{r}) = K_0\sqrt{1 + \frac{\nabla_\perp^2}{(2\pi K_0)^2} + \frac{\sigma\,U(\mathbf{r})}{\pi K_0}}, $$

with $K_0=1/\lambda$ the relativistic wavenumber and $\sigma=2\pi m e\lambda/h^2$ the interaction constant. Within slice $j$ the projected potential $V_j(\mathbf{R})=\int_{(j-1)\Delta z}^{j\Delta z}U(\mathbf{r})\,dz$ defines the slice scattering operator

$$ \hat{K}_j\psi(\mathbf{R}) \;=\; \sigma\,V_j(\mathbf{R})\,\psi(\mathbf{R}) \;+\; \frac{\nabla_\perp^2\psi(\mathbf{R})}{4\pi K_0}. $$

6.2 Dual-Taylor expansion of the slice propagator

The forward propagator over one slice is exponentiated directly:

$$ \psi_{j+1} \;=\; \exp\!\big(i\,\Delta z\,\hat{K}_j\big)\,\psi_j \;=\; \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\,\Delta z)^n}{n!}\,\hat{K}_j^{\,n}\psi_j. $$

The inner power $\hat{K}_j^{\,n}\psi$ is evaluated by a square-root binomial cascade with coefficients

$$ c_1=1,\qquad c_n=\frac{(0.5-n+1)\,\lambda}{\pi\,n},\quad n>1. $$

Linearity of $\hat{K}$ implies $\hat{K}(c\psi)=c\,\hat{K}(\psi)$, so the cascade is implemented in scaled form

$$ \text{cur}_n = c_n\,\hat{K}_j(\text{cur}_{n-1}), \qquad \hat{K}_j^{\,n}\psi \approx \sum_{m=1}^{n}\text{cur}_m, $$

which propagates coefficient damping into every higher-order term and prevents apparent overflow of $\hat{K}_j^{\,n}\psi$ in float32 arithmetic for typical TEM parameters (SrTiO$_3$ at 30 keV, Si at 100 keV).

6.3 Pixel-wise convergence diagnostics

  • Outer divergence ratio (default 5.0): $r = \big(\sum|w|\big)/\big(\sum|\psi_{\text{exit, before}}|\big)$, where $w=\hat{K}^{n}\psi\,(i\Delta z)^n/n!$ and the denominator is evaluated before adding the new term to avoid an artificial bound near 1.
  • Inner stagnation detection: when the number of pixels above threshold ($n_\text{above}$) stops decreasing across consecutive K-iterations, the inner cascade is terminated.
  • Convergence packing. The C++ backend packs $n_\text{above}$, $n_\text{nan}$, and $n_\text{diverging}$ into a single POD struct and copies it to host once per Taylor iteration.

6.4 Bandlimiting

The product $V\psi$ doubles the bandwidth via $\widehat{V\psi}=\hat{V}*\hat\psi$, so unfiltered K-iterations populate frequencies beyond Nyquist. A $\frac{2}{3}$-Nyquist cosine-tapered aperture[6] is applied (i) once to the projected potential before the K expansion and (ii) by default after each $\hat{K}$ application. The FD and FFT Laplacian paths share the same aperture.

6.6 Flux-conserving Fresnel backscattering correction

At the interface between slice $j$ and $j{+}1$, continuity of $\psi$ and $\partial_z\psi$ across the discontinuous $\hat{K}_j$ yields

$$ R = \frac{\hat{K}_j-\hat{K}_{j+1}}{\hat{K}_j+\hat{K}_{j+1}},\qquad T = \frac{2\,\hat{K}_j}{\hat{K}_j+\hat{K}_{j+1}},\qquad |T|^2+|R|^2=1, $$

which we evaluate to first order in $\Delta z\,\nabla V$. The conventional SBA $T=1-B$ with $B=\Delta K/(2K_\text{next})$ does not satisfy unitarity when $B<0$ and is included in the manuscript only as a documented negative control.

6.7 Numerical implementation

  • Public API: CVDMSMultislice(...) in abTEM, called through probe.multislice(potential, algorithm=cvdms).
  • Backend selection: backend="auto" tries C++/CUDA when eligible (CuPy arrays, complex64, $\text{ndim}\ge 2$, fused kernel enabled, valid Laplacian prefactor); otherwise CuPy/Python.
  • The C++/CUDA path fuses Laplacian + $\hat{K}$ + scaled-cascade store + accumulation + convergence counting into one kernel per inner iteration via a templated kseries_iteration_kernel<ACC>. The full-series correction uses fs_fused_kernel<ACC>; BSC dispatch uses two CUDA streams to overlap $V_\text{cur}$ and $V_\text{next}$ K-series work.
  • Default parameters: outer Taylor max 30, inner K-series max 30, $\varepsilon=10^{-7}$, divergence ratio 5.0, stagnation patience 2, FD accuracy 8, AA aperture $\frac{2}{3}$ Nyquist, complex64.

  • float32 heavy-element low-voltage (precision floor).

14.1.2 Language discipline

Use "controlled approximation", "diagnostic", "operating envelope", and "agreement / disagreement with reference" rather than "exact", "universally superior", or "guaranteed". The strongest Comms Phys manuscript makes cautious, falsifiable claims and supplies the evidence chain.
